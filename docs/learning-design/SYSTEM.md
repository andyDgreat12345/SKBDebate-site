# SKB teaching system — design decision v1

10 October 2026. This is the implementation contract for the educational redesign. It supersedes ambiguous recommendations in the earlier roadmap. It is not a claim of educator validation, a deployed feature, or demonstrated learning gains.

Audience confirmed: beginners across speech and debate formats, taught in English. Age, recruitment setting, and weekly founder capacity remain deployment decisions; they do not prevent specification of the teaching system.

## 1. The educational promise

A learner should become more able to make an understandable contribution, support it honestly, listen fairly, and revise thoughtfully. SKB teaches performance and judgment. It should make the reasoning behind communication visible while giving students repeated opportunities to speak and respond.

The practical philosophy is inquiry: claims need reasons; listeners deserve fair representation; questions can uncover uncertainty; and revision can improve an argument without requiring agreement. We assess these actions, not allegiance to a philosophical school, political view, or preferred debate side.

## 2. What a student actually experiences

A new learner sees one sample and a choice of purpose: explain an idea, prepare to debate, or contribute in class. This changes examples and later recommendations, not a supposed personality profile. All paths begin with the same core skills. No long questionnaire or compulsory public introduction precedes a first useful activity.

The first session asks the student to explain a familiar activity to a newcomer for 30 seconds. The learner can rehearse privately or write first. A short model shows how naming a main point helps a listener. The learner retries, and a listener reports the message they understood. The app distinguishes a private rehearsal, a self-review, and an actual listener observation.

The next session develops a reason for a proposal. Subsequent studios add evidence, paraphrase, objections, questions, values, and integrated performance. These are the eight studios in CURRICULUM.md, not eight unrelated videos.

A learner can pause, replay, read, ask for an easier explanation, contest feedback, or choose a model answer. None of these actions is shameful. Assistance is recorded to interpret the work accurately, not to punish the learner. There are no public ability rankings, mandatory streaks, or invented confidence scores.

## 3. The teaching cycle and its boundaries

| Phase | Learner action | Teacher action | Evidence produced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orient | Understand the task and intended audience | Name one achievable skill and show what a usable attempt looks like | Goal choice; no competence judgment |
| Initial attempt | Try before the lesson | Clarify instructions without supplying the answer | Original work plus exposure/assistance status |
| Model | Watch or read a worked explanation | Reveal the relationship step by step | Instructional exposure only |
| Guided attempt | Apply the idea with help available | Identify one useful next change; offer a hint | Assisted practice with feedback provenance |
| Independent attempt | Try a comparable new task | Keep hints available on request, labeled as assistance | Fresh performance; no false unassisted label |
| Revision | Change one part and explain why | Relate feedback to the actual change | Before/after work and reflection |
| Return | Try a short task later | Revisit a difficulty or extend the skill | Additional evidence of retention/transfer |

Learners may explore out of order. The app simply cannot call a post-exposure attempt a baseline. Formative practice is forgiving and retryable. Evaluation attempts are immutable snapshots; corrections create new attempts rather than altering the original.

## 4. Three teaching layers

**Authored core:** reviewed narration, animation, transcripts, examples, exercises, and anticipated misconceptions. Always available without live AI. This is the minimum functioning course.

**Adaptive coaching:** the AI interprets the response within a lesson contract and proposes one next teaching action. It may explain, give a hint, ask clarification, compare examples, or invite revision. It cannot publish lessons, invent source material, assign permanent competence, or execute arbitrary animation code.

**Human learning community:** the founder facilitates workshops; an assigned reviewer checks selected student work and disputed feedback. Public self-study carries no promise of individual review unless capacity exists. A cohort advertises only real sessions and an actual support schedule.

The layers share lesson and rubric versions. Human corrections override an AI judgment in the learner record, while the original feedback remains identifiable as superseded.

## 5. How the teacher decides what to do

First determine whether the response is interpretable. If not, ask a specific clarification; do not classify short or imperfect English as poor reasoning. Then check for a clearly identifiable goal, relevant support, and an explained connection, using the lesson's approved criteria.

For lesson 02 the priority order is:

1. No submitted performance → clarify or help start; no score.
2. Ambiguous meaning → ask what the learner intends.
3. Clear unsupported certainty → narrow the claim; do not call the broader conclusion false.
4. No identifiable proposal → help state the main point.
5. Repeated or irrelevant support → ask for a separate relevant reason.
6. Relevant reason with missing connection → ask how it supports the proposal.
7. Reasonable alternative → accept its potential and examine its justification.
8. Adequate response → acknowledge the specific strength and move to independent application.

This priority is a reviewable instructional policy, not a keyword detector. The model proposes a category with an exact supporting excerpt from the response. Structural checks verify that excerpt exists; they cannot verify that the interpretation is educationally correct. An uncertain or disputed interpretation routes to clarification or human review.

After two unproductive coaching turns on the same point, offer a worked example and a simpler retry rather than repeating generic hints. The learner can request a model sooner. Viewing a model changes assistance status; it does not block learning or mark failure.

The feedback pattern is: identify what the student actually did → explain one relevant issue → ask for one feasible next action. Do not attach praise when no strength has been observed. Do not ask a leading question whose only function is to force agreement.

## 6. Motion and the teacher's presence

Use a calm instructional voice and persistent objects. A claim stays identifiable while a reason appears, a connection is examined, and a revision is made. The teacher highlights the object being discussed. Motion is used to explain a relationship; it is not a reward or a substitute for explanation.

Authored narration is cached and reviewed. Open-ended answers are text-first; optional generated speech follows once stop/resume works reliably. A human-like avatar is not part of the first release. The learner should experience responsiveness through useful feedback and clear visual attention.

One playback controller owns both sound and scene position. A question pauses it. A response may highlight an approved object, reveal a prepared comparison, or show a short text annotation. The student explicitly resumes. Late or canceled responses cannot alter a new exercise. A text/static mode carries the same learning content.

## 7. Progress without misleading scores

Store observations per skill and task. Use descriptive states: not yet attempted; practiced with support; shown independently on this task; revisited later. These are evidence labels, not permanent ranks or universal mastery.

An independent attempt requires that the relevant task was not already exposed, no answer-generating help was used during it, and the record identifies the performance mode. A learner can receive help and continue; the status changes to supported. Models cannot erase assistance or previous exposure.

Rubric judgments include task, version, reviewer type, evidence, and uncertainty. Never infer oral delivery from written work. Speech recognition can assist transcription, but transcription mistakes must be correctable before reasoning feedback. Automated judgments remain provisional; partner or educator observations have separate provenance.

Do not average listening, reasoning, delivery, and English fluency into a single number. A quiet student, accented speaker, or student using accommodations can reason well. The interface should show the next useful practice and examples of actual improvement.

## 8. Course production and editorial responsibility

Every lesson release contains: skill objective; prerequisites; authored script; scene sequence; allowed tutor actions; example responses; acceptable alternatives; source/rights records; assessment tasks; rubric; accessibility equivalents; and review history.

Sources are chosen for educational fit and permitted use. Software-open-source status does not clear media rights. General concepts can support original teaching; using another instructor's expression, media, exercises, or course structure as an adaptation requires an appropriate basis. Attribution and modifications follow the specific source license.

No automatic publish button for AI drafts. Statuses: draft → instructional review → small learner trial → revised → release. A lesson can return to review when errors are reported. Founder and reviewer names only appear once the relevant work has occurred.

## 9. The sustainable service

The first external offering is an openly described beginner course plus a small facilitated cohort. Core lessons and prepared exercises remain accessible when live AI is unavailable or a usage quota is reached. Advanced event preparation can become a paid offering later, after the foundation is useful and costs are measured.

The founder's continuing work is integral: run sessions, observe difficulties, respond to learner questions, improve materials, and support a partner club. The app makes this work repeatable and accessible. It does not replace the service with a collection of generated videos.

Collect only necessary learner information; keep work private by default. Workshop participation, audio storage, model submission, and public portfolio use are separate decisions. Recruitment and applicable consent arrangements must be resolved for the real learner group before external enrollment.

Evaluate access, performance, and continued usefulness separately. A cohort receiving workshops and app practice can be evaluated as a combined program; improvement cannot automatically be attributed to animation or AI. Maintain counts of missing follow-ups and unsuccessful experiences as well as successes.

## 10. Next implementation boundary

Build the teaching loop for studios 1 and 2 before the larger catalogue. It needs the content contract, predictable authored scenes, bounded coaching, attempt provenance, and an honest progress view. Production accounts, D1 saves, usage controls, and private media handling precede recurring external use.

`lesson-02.package.json` is a concrete content contract, and `lesson-02.review-cases.json` provides authored review cases. They are not wired into the existing player. The contract validator checks consistency only. Live model behavior, physical iPad behavior, and teaching effectiveness remain separate tests.

Acceptance: the same lesson is usable without live AI; a justified alternative receives fair treatment; help does not masquerade as independent performance; the learner can explain and revise a connection; and the system's record accurately identifies what happened.
