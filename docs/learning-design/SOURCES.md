DebateDrills' [terms](https://academy.debatedrills.com/pages/terms) reserve its content and generally limit access to personal, noncommercial use; modifying or redistributing content is restricted absent permission. A free course is not an open adaptation license. Redrawing someone else's course into animation does not automatically resolve its rights.

The [CC BY 4.0 deed](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) allows sharing/adaptation including commercially with its conditions, including credit, a license link, and modification notices. Third-party items and other rights can still require separate treatment. This is a production rule for selecting material, not a blanket legal clearance of any collection.

## Recommended content strategy

Use original scripts and examples as the main curriculum, informed by study of established teaching. Adapt particular open materials where doing so adds value and permissions are clear. A named workshop partnership is an optional route for later modules; do not make the first release dependent on receiving a reply.

For personal development, the founder can first study the UW introductory course and DebateDrills argument-structure course, taking notes on learner difficulties, practice design, and feedback. Do the exercises personally. Course completion should be represented accurately; it is not a teaching qualification or reuse license.

## A repeatable rights record

Every production source gets: ID; title; creator; exact URL/edition/page or timestamp; access date; intended use; license/permission basis; exceptions; required attribution; changes; reviewer; and status.

Statuses: reference only → candidate → asset-level review complete → approved for specified use. Unknown stays reference only. Distinguish adapting a source, quoting a short passage, linking externally, and using an original example about a general concept.

For each animation credit screen and Sources panel, record the actual authors and adaptations. Example format, to fill only after the chosen section is checked:

> Adapted from [title, section] by [creators], [source URL], licensed [license URL]. Changes: original school examples, shortened explanation, new diagrams, and interactive exercises. No endorsement implied.

Do not inherit a teacher's voice, image, school branding, or students' recordings from a source license without checking the relevant permissions. Use an ordinary licensed synthetic voice or the founder's consented narration.

## Optional partnership brief — prepared, not sent

Propose a pilot adapting one specifically identified lesson into two short animated segments plus original exercises. Ask about permitted media, commercial/noncommercial use, attribution, editing, AI-assisted production, use in tutor context, approval of the adaptation, duration, and any fee. Do not request an entire private course archive as a starting point.

Acceptance produces a written permission record. No reply means we continue with original or clearly licensed material. Any future revenue-sharing arrangement needs explicit terms; no such arrangement is assumed here.

## Learning-design references and limits

- [Brame (2016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5132380/): review of video learning considerations. Informs short segments, signaling, and active tasks; not a demonstration that our animations improve debate outcomes. Refer to its ideas with attribution; do not adapt its NC-SA figures into commercial lessons without checking permissions.
- [IES practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1): spacing, examples alongside practice, and explanatory questions. Evidence is general and varies by recommendation. Our exact sequence and timing remain hypotheses.
- [H5P Interactive Video](https://h5p.org/interactive-video), [Branching Scenario](https://h5p.org/branching-scenario), and [licensing](https://h5p.org/licensing): existing authored interaction capabilities to benchmark; software licensing is separate from media rights.

NSDA Learn could not be reliably fetched during this research. It remains a future format-rule/resource check, not a claimed inspected curriculum. No source above is described as an SKB partner.
