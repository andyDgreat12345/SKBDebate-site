# SKB: learning experience and operating blueprint

Decision draft · 10 October 2026 · researched and designed with AI assistance; no educator approval or learner outcomes claimed.

This plan supersedes the earlier feature-first roadmap. It does not change the live site. The existing lab is a technical experiment, not a finished course.

## Decisions and assumptions

Confirmed by the founder: English; beginners across speech and debate formats; animated explanations with useful teacher interaction; accessible core education; potential commercialization later.

Working assumptions, to validate: secondary-school learners, a small supervised pilot, students with varied English proficiency, and access through phones/tablets as well as computers. Learner ages, geography, founder availability, reviewer availability, budget, and recruitment partner remain unknown. Do not treat these assumptions as permission to enroll children or launch a paid service.

The educational promise: **prepare a short, understandable contribution; give reasons for it; listen accurately; answer a fair question; and improve after feedback.**

This common foundation serves public speaking and debate. It does not claim to train every competitive event: interpretation, performance, PF, LD, policy, and World Schools need later event-specific teaching and qualified review.

## What makes this educationally different

The unit of progress is a student performance followed by a revision. Watching a video is preparation for that work. Each studio combines approximately 3–4 minutes of authored animation, short thinking pauses, 5–8 minutes of speaking or writing, and feedback. Those durations are design hypotheses to test, not an evidence-backed optimum.

The proposed first program has eight studios across four weeks and one 45-minute human-led workshop each week. Estimated learner commitment is 70–95 minutes weekly, depending on retries. A self-paced path offers the same practice but does not promise human feedback unless a reviewer is assigned.

The app handles repeatable explanation and practice. The founder runs workshops, notices misconceptions, reviews examples, and improves the curriculum. A second experienced coach checks instructional quality. AI supports production and bounded feedback; it does not confer teaching credentials or replace this review.

## What research changed

- The University of Washington public-speaking syllabus makes actual speech production central. Our initial course must include oral practice, not only written rebuttals. [Course](https://www.coursera.org/learn/public-speaking)
- NSD describes instruction connected to small-group practice and mentorship. Our operating model therefore includes facilitated practice rather than relying on passive viewing. This is a design inference from a provider's description, not proof of our program's effectiveness. [Program](https://www.nsdebatecamp.com/public-forum-online)
- ESU organizes oracy broadly enough to include listening and delivery. Our common foundation must not collapse all speech education into argument logic. [Resources](https://www.esu.org/resources/)
- Educational-video guidance emphasizes managing cognitive load and active learning. We will use motion to expose relationships and pause for student decisions, with captions and text alternatives. It does not establish that more animation produces more learning. [Brame, 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5132380/)
- The IES practice guide supports spacing, worked examples alongside problems, and explanatory questions, with different evidence levels across recommendations. We will test these principles in our context, not claim they validate this specific app. [Guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1)

## What is wrong with the current prototype

| Current behavior | Educational problem | Required correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone-policy lesson followed by cycling/health inference | The transfer task introduces causal-selection reasoning not explicitly taught | Align each assessment to taught skills; teach causation in a later unit |
| Correct multiple-choice option unlocks revision | Recognition is treated as readiness; a defensible alternative may be blocked | Allow revision independently; use choices as formative hints |
| Text-only student work | Cannot establish speaking or listening improvement | Add live oral practice first, then optional private audio |
| Generic open question box | The tutor can discuss a topic without diagnosing a learning need | Give it current objective, response, rubric, and approved lesson context |
| Self-check boxes and completion | These record activity and self-perception, not demonstrated competence | Keep performance evidence and human rubric judgments separate |
| Starting attempt can occur after exploration | It is not reliably a pre-instruction baseline | Track exposure; keep diagnostic exercises separate from open exploration |
| Tab-only storage and JSON export | Not a reliable learner portfolio | Add authenticated, versioned saves before a recurring external pilot |
| Three emphasis states | Motion changes focus but does not explain how reasoning develops | Use authored sequences that construct, compare, and revise ideas |

Keep the lab as a sandbox. Do not polish its present content into a claimed course without these changes.

## The first release and later app

Release 1 is a small course with Learn, Practice, Portfolio, and a Workshop page. Resources have explicit audience, author, source, permission status, and last review. Educator tools begin as a private review queue; a full CMS is unnecessary for the first few lessons.

Later, the shared foundation branches into public-speaking and competition tracks. The recovered mock-round system can become advanced practice after its source and feedback quality are evaluated. Community sharing begins with moderated submissions and workshop cohorts. Open messaging, public student recordings, payments, and unrestricted AI voice sessions each create separate operational responsibilities and are outside the first release.

## The next work is concrete, but is not another site expansion

1. Review the worked lesson with the founder and a coach; record corrections in its revision log.
2. Deliver its script and exercise manually to three volunteer beginners. Record only consented observations; do not market this as a study proving efficacy.
3. Produce one 60–90-second motion segment plus one meaningful checkpoint from the revised script.
4. Test whether learners can explain the connection better after the segment, and whether the interface works on the actual iPad/network.
5. Build two complete studios only after the instructional pattern is understandable.
6. Add accounts and saving, then run the two-studio pilot. Expand the curriculum based on observed difficulties.

No content provider has been contacted, no class has been purchased or completed on the founder's behalf, and no third-party workshop has been copied into the app. The linked sources were researched through public pages and accessible text; details are in the source ledger.
